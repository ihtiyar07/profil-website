## 1. Context
Industrial telemetry, IoT sensor networks, and transaction streams generate tens of thousands of inserts every second. As time-series data accumulates, traditional relational tables grow into hundreds of millions and billions of rows.

This project engineered a scalable TimescaleDB storage engine architecture that sustains heavy insert throughput, accelerates analytical aggregation queries from minutes to sub-20ms, and radically reduces disk footprint.

## 2. The Problem: Standard RDBMS Bottlenecks

Attempting to store billions of time-series records in vanilla PostgreSQL tables causes three major architectural failures:
1. **Index Bloat and Buffer Eviction:** As tables expand, B-Tree indexes exceed the server's `shared_buffers` (RAM). New inserts trigger random disk page reads to update indexes (I/O thrashing). Write throughput degrades from 30,000 writes/sec to a fraction of that capacity.
2. **Exhaustive Analytical Scans:** Calculating hourly or daily aggregations over millions of rows forces sequential scans (`Seq Scan`), locking CPU cores and starving disk bandwidth.
3. **DELETE Bloat and Vacuum Lockouts:** Clearing outdated data via `DELETE FROM table WHERE time < ...` creates massive dead tuple bloat in PostgreSQL's MVCC engine; subsequent `VACUUM` operations lock disk resources for hours.

## 3. Architecture and Storage Optimizations

### A. RAM Buffer Cache-Centric Chunk Sizing
Data was partitioned into TimescaleDB hypertables under a strict architectural rule: **"Active chunks and indexes must not consume more than 25% of available server RAM."**
- Chunk intervals were mathematically calibrated to 7-day windows based on ingest volume and available `shared_buffers`.
- Time-bounded queries leverage partition pruning: the query planner touches only target chunks, bypassing hundreds of gigabytes of historical data on disk.

### B. Materialized Continuous Aggregates
Hourly, daily, and monthly rollup views were configured with `WITH (timescaledb.continuous)`:
- Background worker threads asynchronously aggregate incoming records into pre-calculated summaries.
- Fetching 6-month historical trends does not scan billions of raw points; it reads directly from materialized summaries in &lt;18ms.

### C. Zstd Columnar Compression and Zero-Lock Retention
- Chunks older than 14 days are converted from row-based storage into columnar compression utilizing the Zstandard algorithm. A 280GB raw table collapsed to 22.4GB (92% space savings).
- Historical records beyond 180 days are pruned via automated `drop_chunks()` policies. This drops filesystem blocks directly without generating MVCC dead tuples, executing in milliseconds with zero transaction locking.
